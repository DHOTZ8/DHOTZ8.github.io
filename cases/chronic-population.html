01 · Business question

How can unstructured clinical text support population analysis?

Clinical descriptions can contain abbreviations, diagnosis codes and inconsistent language. I developed a dictionary-based processing script to standardize these inputs and assign disease categories for subsequent population analysis.

02 · My approach

01

Normalize the inputs

Load reference dictionaries for diagnosis codes, abbreviations and disease terms. Standardize clinical text using Python and regular expressions.

02

Apply classification rules

Map recognized terms to disease categories. Preserve multiple category matches when they occur in the same record.

03

Prepare analytical outputs

Export cleaned text and matched categories to support review, segmentation and reporting.

03 · Analytical view

Making the approach visible.

The chart is a conceptual illustration of population segmentation. It is not a computed cluster analysis, clinical risk score or result from the original project.

04 · Decision relevance

From analysis to context.

Structured categories make it possible to review population profiles and explore utilization or cost patterns. Record-level validation remains essential before drawing conclusions.

Interpretation & limitations

Term matching needs validation: negation, family history and ambiguous abbreviations can produce false matches. This is a rule-based analytical aid, not a clinical diagnosis model. A public demonstration is provided below with synthetic text.
